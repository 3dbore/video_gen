## Pacing
The source runs 8 scenes in 14.05s: median 1.61s, shortest 1.27s, longest 2.38s, 5.0 scene changes per 10s. It works because each scene carries exactly one idea, so the viewer never has to choose what to read.

This film is 50s, following its 47.68s of narration, in the brief's six beats (5.4, 7.3, 9.5, 15.7, 5, 7.1s), so a cut every 1.6s would fight the voice. Scaled: the six beats are the scenes, and inside a beat something new lands every 1.3 to 2.4s, taken from the source's scene lengths and timed to the words being said. That gives about 4 landings per 10s and 5 hard cuts in the film. Like the source, the pace slows in the middle (Beat 4 is the longest) and tightens towards the logo. The exception is the hero number, which holds longest.

## First motion
In the source, the first change comes at 0.35s on an empty field. The eye gets motion before it gets content, so it follows the first line as it draws. Here the first line of the robot arm starts drawing by 0.3s on the navy field, and the arm is recognisable by 1.5s. Beat 1 gives the arm's joint-bend to the word "gratis", so nothing else competes with it.

## Cut rhythm
Hard cuts only, and only on the 5 beat changes. The source flips its field on every scene change, which makes each cut read instantly, even when the type is small. Here each cut changes the field colour too: Beat 1 navy, Beat 2 white, Beat 3 cobalt, Beat 4 navy, Beat 5 white, Beat 6 cobalt. Between cuts, change comes from elements landing, not from new shots. The rhythm changes at Beat 4, where landings stop while the hero number holds, then again at Beat 5, which the brief makes slower.

## Look
See design.md. Palette, type, radius, spacing and the logo all come from it, using the brief's five colours.
Layout habits taken from the source: one idea per field, and strong scale contrast between the big line and its small label, with the middle sizes left out. A key word is marked by weight. Text never crops at the frame edge, since this is watched on a phone. The smallest text stays readable at phone size.

## Sound
No music, as the brief says, and effects are CC0 only. The voice carries the film, with sparse effects placed only where something lands: one small motor sound on the joint bend, a soft click on the licence card, one tick per box, a soft tone in Beat 5 and a short rising sound on the logo. At the key moment, ticks speed up as the number climbs and stop dead when it lands, so the silence underlines the price. The move-then-hold idea comes from the sound density measured in 5f8ab1bd28bf48862352e5eacd86b60b.mp4, used here as a principle only.

## Text behavior
The Beat 1 title is already in place on frame 0, so the thumbnail is never empty. Elsewhere, lines enter from opposite sides and converge where a beat has two lines, as "Hello" and "world!" do in the source, because the meeting point draws the eye to the line as a whole. Type holds until the next cut once it lands, except where the brief gives its own entry: the Beat 2 credit types out letter by letter, the Beat 2 card rises from below, the first number climbs and the second number enters from the side. Scale hierarchy: one large line per field, labels far smaller.

## Easing feel
Snappy out, without bounce on ordinary type: about 0.45s, power2.out, settled in under 0.8s, matching the measured x move (0.467s, 0% overshoot). The only hold-then-snap in the film is the hero landing. The MG90S range plate's echo copies are the one loud gesture, and they make the number feel heavier than anything before it.

## Payoff
Two moments. The film's point lands in Beat 4, when the number climbs to Rp22.500, then on to Rp71.745, and the full range stops on a chartreuse plate with navy text. Ticks stop dead and the echo stack collapses to one plate. The source's stack is the device, and it appears nowhere else. Its ending lands in Beat 6, the stillest scene of the film, with the logo, line and contact stacked and at rest, as in the source's last 1.27s.

## Things NOT to copy
Any clip's wording, footage, logo, flower glyph or watermark. Black and white, green, purple or any other clip's palette. Italic type (Geom only, in its listed styles). The mirrored-text flip, the word wheel, the type wall, words cropped off the frame, and a logo pulled apart.

## Must match
- First motion before 0.3s: the arm's first line is drawing on the navy field.
- Exactly 5 hard cuts, on the beat changes, and the field colour changes at every one.
- One idea per field: each still has one large line, and everything else is clearly smaller.
- Ordinary type settles in under 0.8s with no visible bounce.
- A new element lands at least every 2.4s, except during the hero hold.
- The MG90S range sits on a chartreuse plate with navy text, shows an echo stack within 0.3s of landing, and is one plate by 1.0s after landing.
- The MG90S range holds still for longer than any other element holds before a change, and it stays larger than the MG996R range.
