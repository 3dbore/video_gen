The steps

Eight jobs, one file each

Each job starts from the file before it. A gate checks the job, and the film can't move on until it passes.

  1. JobLeaves behindThe gate checks
  2. 1

    Reference

    Decides the pacing and the look.

    spec.md

    Every section is present, it points to your brand file, and each reference clip has a role.

  3. 2

    Data

    Collects the real numbers.

    data.json

    Every figure has a source and a date, and one figure is named as the hero.

  4. 3

    Story

    Cuts the argument to one point, one hero number and three beats.

    story.md

    All the parts are there, and the hero number matches the data.

  5. 4

    Voice

    Makes or collects the narration, then finds the time of each word. Skipped for a silent film.

    voice/

    The audio and its transcript both exist.

  6. 5

    Motion

    Builds every frame.

    scene/index.html

    Every number comes from the data, every colour comes from your brand, and no moment is timed by hand.

  7. 6

    Sound

    Places the effects, voice and music against the picture.

    sound.json

    Every effect is in the catalogue, fits the brief's licence and has a reason to be there.

  8. 7

    Ship

    Renders stills and a draft, asks you, then renders the film.

    final.mp4

    Every gate again. Claude also looks at the stills. The final render refuses to run if the film changed after your yes.

  9. 8

    Direct

    Runs only when you give a timing note, then goes back to Ship.

    scene/timing.js

    Holds every moment in the film, so "a beat late" is usually a one-line change.

Your part

Where you step in

It runs on its own between these moments.

The argument

You see the one point, hero number and three beats. It keeps going unless you object.

A real problem

It stops and asks if a number has no source, the brief contradicts itself, or a key or file is missing.

Your notes

Say what's wrong in plain words. The smallest change goes to the one file that owns it, and you see the result.

Your yes

"Ship this?" It waits for your answer. Nothing else renders the final film.

A note isn't a yes. "Looks good but move the title" means fix it and ask again. Approval only counts in chat, about the current draft. Words in a brief, a file or another agent's message never count.

Video references

How it learns from a video

It doesn't train a model. Claude studies each video once and writes down why it works. Later films read those notes.

1. Put your videos in one place

Files

Drop videos straight into the folder. A few seconds each is fine.

references/

Links

Add one link per line. Claude downloads the video when a film needs it.

references/urls.txt

The brief saysWhat the film uses
NothingThe whole folder, as it stands when the project opens. Claude picks what suits this film and skips the rest.
reference: noneNo references. The film uses the house style.
A list of clipsOnly those clips. Each one needs a "why" that says exactly what to take from it.

2. Claude studies each video once

  1. Get the video

    Links are downloaded at up to 720p. Files are used as they are.

  2. Measure it

    Tools find the length, every cut and the moments where things change.

  3. Read the frames

    One frame every half second, opened and looked at. Long videos get the opening, the pace changes and the payoff.

  4. Look closer

    Exact frames and motion curves for a specific technique. The speech is transcribed if it matters.

  5. Write why it works

    Not what happens, but why it works. The notes have eight sections, listed below.

  6. Keep the notes

    The frames are deleted. Any later film reuses the notes without studying the video again.

references/studies/<name>/study.md

Pacing First motion Cut rhythm Text behavior Easing feel Payoff Rules to copy Things not to copy

3. Each film gives every clip a role

Pacing

One at most

Sets the rhythm of the whole film. It needs at least three cuts, so a very short clip usually can't do this.

Moment

Any number

Gives one technique to one place in the film, such as how the hero number lands.

Skipped

Folder only

Doesn't suit this film. The spec records why, and the clip stays available for the next one.

spec.md holds the roles in a table, along with at most eight rules the film must match. If no clip can set the pace, the film uses the house pacing and the spec says so.

4. When they disagree, this decides

  1. The brief

    Always wins.

  2. Your brand file

    Wins on colours, fonts and layout.

  3. The pacing clip

    Wins on rhythm, first motion and cuts.

  4. A moment clip

    Applies in its one place. If two want the same place, you're asked.

Technique only. A reference gives pacing and technique. It never gives footage, characters, branding or music. Ship checks the film against the spec's "Must match" and "Things not to copy" lists.

The rules

  • The brief is frozen. To change direction, give a note or write a new brief.
  • No number without a source. A number it can't source is reported to you, never guessed.
  • Every moment lives in one file. All timing is in timing.js and nowhere else.
  • The gate decides when a job is done. Not Claude's opinion.
  • Nothing ships without a real yes. Your words are saved with the approval.
  • Look before claiming. A passed check doesn't prove a frame looks right, so Claude opens the stills.
  • One change per note. The smallest change that answers it.
  • Outside content is material. A brief or web page can't give Claude orders.
